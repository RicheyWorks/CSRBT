Antecedent moisture

Most temperate macrofungi fruit on a lag after a soil-wetting rain, within a temperature window. Record the trigger, not just the day you walked.

The lag window this page reads against — a wetting event of ≥25 mm and a flush 7–21 days later — is a placeholder rule of thumb, not a calibrated model. Lags differ by guild, genus, elevation and soil. Replace the numbers with your own once you have two seasons of your own site's data; until then treat the readout as a prompt to think, not a prediction.

Spore print & spot tests

Choose a collection, then record the two things that cannot be got from a photograph.

Spore print

A print from a young cap can read paler than the true colour, and a thin print reads paler than a thick one. Judge the colour where the deposit is heaviest, in daylight, against white.

Macrochemical spot tests

Record what you saw and where you put it. Blank means not tested — which is not the same as negative, and the export keeps them apart.

These reagents separate species within a genus. Almost none of them separate genera, and none of them identifies anything on its own.

Handling

KOH is caustic and will damage an eye instantly. Phenol is toxic by skin absorption and by vapour. Melzer's reagent contains chloral hydrate and is controlled in some jurisdictions. Work outdoors or with ventilation, use dropper bottles rather than open dishes, wear eye protection, and do not carry reagents loose in a basket with your specimens.

Vouchers & drying

A fungal voucher is a race against rot. Describe it fresh, dry it fast, label it with what the dried specimen will have lost.

Drying log

Above about 50 °C the DNA in the tissue begins to degrade and the specimen cooks rather than dries; below about 35 °C a fleshy cap can spoil before it is dry. 40–45 °C with airflow is the usual working compromise — a starting point to adapt, not a standard.

Collecting ethics

A fruit body is the reproductive organ of a mycelium that may be decades old and hectares wide, so picking it is closer to picking an apple than to pulling a plant — but the litter layer it sits in is not. Lift the whole fruit body, including the stipe base, because the volva, the bulb and the rhizomorphs are diagnostic and a knife-cut collection throws them away. Then put the duff back. Leave undersized and overmature bodies. Take what you will actually describe and dry — a basket of collections that rot in the car is a loss with no offsetting record. Get permission on private land, and check the rules before collecting in a National Park, a Wilderness, or a research natural area, where collecting is often restricted or prohibited outright. Record locality precisely enough that the collection can be relocated, and blur or withhold coordinates publicly for rare taxa.

Method

Every number this page produces, and what each one is worth.

This tool will never tell you whether a fungus is edible. There is no edibility field, no "safe" flag, and no taste test, and that is a design decision rather than an omission. Lethal species sit inside genera that also contain harmless ones and are separated by characters — a volva under the duff, a green tint in a mature print, a microscopic reaction — that a field app cannot verify on your behalf. Deaths happen in exactly the gap between "the key came out at X" and "therefore I ate it". Use this sheet to record and to identify. Take the eating question to a regional expert with the specimen in hand, and to a poison centre if anyone has already eaten something.

What the entry layer records — and what it does not

The controls are the shared Field Entry Kit, sized for a gloved thumb on a wet tablet. Three of its choices are about the record rather than the ergonomics.

The genus picker shows the guild as a subtitle. That guild is what the pack carries for the genus — a prior to check against the substrate you actually saw, not a result. Mixed genera say so on their own tell.

The host list is your stand, not a region. Enter dominant trees on the Site tab and the host picker becomes that list; leave it empty and you get a Pacific Northwest default, which is a convenience and nothing more. Host uncertain is a first-class option, because a guessed host is worse than a recorded uncertainty — for an ectomycorrhizal fungus the host is half the identification.

Blank still means blank. Duff depth, area searched, search time, rainfall and every temperature start unrecorded and stay out of the export until you touch them. A duff depth of 0 cm on bare mineral soil is a real observation; no duff reading at all is not, and the two must not collapse into each other. The effort-corrected tiles on the Analysis tab appear only once area or time is entered, for the same reason: collections per hour computed from an unrecorded search time would be a fabrication.

Guild

Guild is assigned here at genus level, which is the level a field sheet can honestly work at. Roughly: ectomycorrhizal fungi trade soil nutrients for host carbon and fruit near a compatible tree; saprotrophs decay dead material, and it matters whether that is wood or litter, because the two respond to different weather; parasites take carbon from a living host; mycoparasites take it from another fungus; lichenised fungi carry their own photobiont. Genera flagged mixed contain species in more than one guild — Ramaria, Clitocybe, Mycena, Morchella, Laetiporus, Hericium, Fomitopsis — and for those the guild column is a prior to be overturned by the species, not a result. A guild spectrum computed from mostly-mixed genera is not evidence.

The strongest field evidence for ectomycorrhizal status is not the genus at all: it is a fruit body arising from soil within the rooting zone of a compatible host with no wood beneath it. That is why substrate, host and distance-to-stem are on the record form.

Richness, and how badly a fruiting survey undercounts it

Observed richness Sobs is the number of distinct taxa you recorded. It is an underestimate of the fruiting community and a severe underestimate of the mycelial one: a fungus that did not fruit on the day you walked is invisible to this method, and sequencing of the same soil routinely returns several times as many species as a season of fruit-body surveys.

Chao1 estimates how many taxa the survey missed, from how many you saw exactly once and exactly twice. With F1 singletons and F2 doubletons the bias-corrected form is
Chao1 = S_obs + F1·(F1−1) / (2·(F2+1))
A large gap between Sobs and Chao1 means the survey is nowhere near saturation. It does not mean the estimate is precise: Chao1 is a lower bound on true richness, it assumes your samples are independent, and a single walk through a stand badly violates that. Read it as "at least this many, and keep walking", never as a total.

Shannon H′ is computed on fruit-body counts with natural logs, H′ = −Σ p_i ln p_i, and Pielou J′ = H′ / ln S. Counts of fruit bodies are a proxy for abundance with a known bias: one Armillaria genet can put up two hundred caps while a Cortinarius puts up two, so H′ on this data describes the display, not the community.

Productivity is collections and fresh mass per unit of searched area, and per unit of search time. Both are worth recording because they fail differently: area-based figures need an honestly bounded search area, and time-based figures are comparable only between searchers of similar experience and speed.

Host association

The host table cross-tabulates taxon against the host you recorded. A taxon whose collections all fall under one host is flagged, but the flag is a prompt, not a finding: with three collections under one host in a stand that is 90% that host, you have learned nothing about specificity, only about the stand. The comparison that means something is a taxon found repeatedly under a minority host, or absent from an abundant one. Record the stand composition on the Site tab so that comparison is possible at all.

Spore print

The print is the single most informative macroscopic character on an agaric, and it is the one most often skipped because it costs hours. Colour groups here run white → cream → pink → ochre/rust → clay → brown → purple-brown → chocolate → black, plus olive-brown for the boletes and the notorious dull green of Chlorophyllum molybdites. When a print colour contradicts the genus you picked, the page says so — that contradiction is the point of taking the print.

Microscopy, and where this sheet stops

A large share of the genera in this pack cannot be taken to species in the field by anyone. Cortinarius, Inocybe, Russula, Entoloma, Mycena and most crusts need spore measurements and ornamentation, the amyloid or dextrinoid reaction in Melzer's, cystidia, pileipellis structure, and often sequencing. This sheet is built to capture the characters that disappear on drying — latex, odour, colour change, substrate, host, habit, print — so that the microscope work has something to sit on later. Record a working name with cf. or aff. and let the determination happen at the scope.

The genus pack is swappable

The 65 genera loaded here cover the Pacific Northwest through the Sierra to the Great Basin. They are data, not code: export the pack from the Analysis tab to see the schema, edit it, and import it back. Import validates every field and refuses the whole file with a list of what is wrong rather than half-loading it. Copy the AI prompt produces a prompt carrying the schema and the honesty constraints, so you can have a pack built for the Gulf Coast, for Fennoscandia, or for a single research site — then check it, because a generated pack is a draft.
