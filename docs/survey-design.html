The shape of the survey

A survey is not a flat list of records. It is a season containing sites, containing plots, containing visits — and the file you deposit has to carry that shape or nobody can tell which numbers are comparable to which.

Dataset

The prefix makes every eventID unique within this dataset, which is what Darwin Core requires. It does not make them unique in the world — if you expect to merge with another dataset, use something nobody else will pick.

Photographs

A survey design is a claim about places. The photograph is what says the place was what the design assumed — that the plot was in the habitat it was stratified into, that the transect was walkable, that the absence was a look and not a gap.

The export carries the reference, not the image. The frames stay in this tab and are never uploaded; closing it discards them, and the originals are in your camera roll. What travels is the filename, a CRC-32 and when the file was taken — enough to put a row and a frame back together later. The CRC is an integrity check, not a cryptographic one.

Taxonomic scope

Multiple values are separated with a pipe (|), which is the Humboldt Extension's convention. Excluded scope is not a formality. “We surveyed vascular plants and deliberately did not record mosses” and “we surveyed everything and found no mosses” produce the same empty cells and mean opposite things.

Method

Why a flat table is not enough

Simple Darwin Core — the format the Relevé, Stand Sheet and Collection Sheet export — is one flat table of things that were found. It is exactly right for that, and it has two things it structurally cannot say.

The first is structure. Twelve plots visited three times each is thirty-six sampling events nested inside twelve locations nested inside one season. Flattened, every row repeats the plot and the season, and nothing records that these three rows were the same plot on different days rather than three plots.

The second is absence. A flat occurrence table contains what you found. It has no way of saying I looked for this and it was not there, which for anything to do with change over time is the more valuable half of the record. Event Core gives you dwc:eventID and dwc:parentEventID for the structure; the Humboldt Extension for Ecological Inventories gives you the terms that make an absence legible.

The declaration that makes an absence mean something

An occurrence row with occurrenceStatus = absent and nothing else is not data. Absent from what search? Was the observer looking for it? Would they have recognised it? Without an answer, a reader cannot distinguish a genuine absence from a gap in the survey, and the safe reading is to discard the row.

Three Humboldt terms carry the answer, and this page will not emit an absence without them:

  • eco:isAbsenceReported — absences are reported in this event at all.
  • eco:targetTaxonomicScope — the group you were searching for.
  • eco:excludedTaxonomicScope — what you deliberately did not look at.
The gate: declare a target scope and this page writes your absences. Leave it blank and it refuses, because the row it would write could not be read by anybody, including you in two years.

Nothing is inherited

The Humboldt Extension is explicit, and it is the rule most implementations get wrong: “A child dwc:Event MUST NOT be assumed to implicitly ‘inherit’ the value of any property of any of its parent dwc:Events; rather, the value SHOULD be provided explicitly.”

So the intuitive economy — write the coordinates once on the site and let the plots below it inherit — produces a file in which the plots have no coordinates. This page writes every applicable value at every level where it applies, which makes the file larger and makes it correct. That is the Principle of Applicability, and the redundancy is the point.

What this page will not fill in

eco:inventoryTypes takes a value from a controlled vocabulary describing how the searching was organised. The published term list this page was built from defines the term but does not publish the value list, and this page will not guess at one — a controlled value invented here would validate against nothing and would be worse than a free-text note that is honestly free text. So the field is free text, and the export says so.
