# ADR-193 — The brief: a goal that hands over what it will mark

**Status:** accepted · **Date:** 2026-09-12 · **The sixth of the seven slices of `docs/PLAN-operator-api-2026-09-11.md`. The third blind trial graded four operators against what their TASKS hold and handed them what their GOALS say, and the two are not the same set: across the 21 science tasks, 547 values entered and a majority of them appearing nowhere in the goal that was handed over. A goal becomes a brief — `says` (the prose, unchanged), `gives` (every value the task supplies, with the control that takes it) and `holds` (every reading it holds, with its claims) — both new parts computed from the steps rather than typed. `goal_of` and `brief_of` on the command line, a blind brief that hands over the data and withholds the answers, and two numbers on the board: **642 values handed over**, 632 readings and 1 956 claims held. New suite `verify_brief` 31, new runner `mutate_brief` 14/14.**

## 1. An unfair trial is not a hard one

ADR-187's post-mortem said it in one line and then moved on:

> **THE MISSES ARE THE TASKS' FAULT** — they hold things their goals never say
> (32 mm rain event, Bear Creek, "Cap viscid when wet").

That is worth a number, so here it is. For each of the 21 science tasks, take
every value the task enters and ask whether the goal sentence handed to the
operator contains it:

    547 values entered
    281 of them appearing nowhere in the goal          51%

Half the data was withheld from the people being marked on it. An operator
cannot reason its way to "Bear Cr. old-growth" or to `45.3736`; it can only be
told. Grading someone on a figure computed from a value they were never given
measures the grader, not the operator.

## 2. A goal is three things now

    says    the prose, unchanged -- what this is for and what to do
    gives   every value the task supplies, with the control that takes it
    holds   every reading the task holds, with its claims (ADR-187)

`says` is untouched: not one of the 55 goal sentences was rewritten, because
the sentence was never the problem. What was missing was everything around it.

**`gives` and `holds` are derived from the steps, never typed.** That is the
whole of why they can be trusted. A brief written *beside* a task goes stale
the first time the task is edited, and a stale brief is worse than none: it is
a promise about data the operator will not find. The suite holds that no task
file carries a brief of its own, and that a step added to a task is in its
brief at once, with the control it is pointed at.

    $ python3 tools/harness_tasks.py --brief page-pheno-tracker-science --blind

    TARGET: page  (pheno-tracker.html)
    RUNGS:  SENSITIVE_READ, DRAFT, MUTATE

    ## What this is for
    On the pheno tracker: name and stamp a run of 8, score every plant …

    ## The data to enter (16 value(s), 0 of them bulk)
        runName                  set-text  "Blueberry F2"
        runObs                   set-text  "R. Ellison"
        runDate                  set-text  "2026-09-08"
        plants in run            set-text  "8"
        …
    ## What it holds
        12 reading(s) and 32 claim(s), withheld: this is a blind brief.

The controls are named the page's way — `runName`, `plants in run` — because
that is what ADR-128's grammar already made possible and what a reader can
act on. A value long enough to be copied rather than retyped (a CSV block, an
imported document, a paragraph of field notes) is marked `bulk` and printed
whole, so a brief never pretends a sentence could carry it.

## 3. Blind is what a trial opts into

`brief_of(task)` gives the full brief. `brief_of(task, claims=False)` hands
over every value and withholds what the task holds, saying so — because an
operator told the answers is being asked to transcribe rather than to operate.

The default is the full one, and that is deliberate: an organiser who forgets
the flag gets a brief with the answers in it and *notices*, rather than running
a trial that was accidentally fair and silently useless. The suite holds the
default.

## 4. What the brief holds is what the grader scores

    what a brief HOLDS  ==  what grade_outcomes scores
    step for step, claim for claim, for all 55 tasks

A brief that promised a reading the grader did not want, or wanted one the
brief did not promise, would make a host that satisfied it fail anyway. Held
against the real thing too: each of ADR-187's four traces is re-graded and the
grader's rows are exactly the brief's, in order.

So `holds` is a self-grading target. A host driving a task it has been given
can check itself as it goes, and know that satisfying the brief is the same
thing as scoring full marks.

## 5. Not a gateway action, and that is the point

The plan said "with `goal()` exposed", and it is — on the command line and as a
library function, and **not** as a tool behind the door.

A door that served a client its own task would end the blind trial that made
the brief necessary. ADR-136's discipline is that `tools/tasks/`, the traces,
the ledger and every ADR are *removed from the filesystem* the operator works
in; a `csrbt_page__goal` tool would hand back through the door exactly what was
deleted from the disk. The suite holds that no transport and no plugin imports
`harness_tasks`.

The brief is for the trial's **organiser**, and for a host that has been *given*
a task by someone who wanted it done.

## 6. Where the checks live

`verify_brief` is its own suite rather than another section of `verify_tasks`,
and the reason is arithmetic. `verify_tasks` drives real browsers and takes
eight minutes; not one of these checks opens anything, and the whole suite runs
in **0.3 seconds**. A mutant runner over an eight-minute suite is twelve hours
for fourteen mutants and therefore never run, which is the same as having no
runner.

## Held

- `gives` covers every action that SUPPLIES something, and the suite keeps its
  own copy of that list rather than reading the subject's: a check that asks
  the code what it found agrees with it by construction, and an action dropped
  from one table would be dropped from the other in the same breath.
- The environment is data. A figure that came out of a seeded draw or a frozen
  clock is not reachable by an operator who was not told the seed, so
  `set-seed`, `set-clock` and `set-dialog` are things a task gives.
- A ledger row says what the brief says, through one function used by both the
  run's row and the could-not-stand-up's — so a task's counts do not depend on
  whether its target came up that morning.
- Not one goal sentence, page or task step was edited. The 55 tasks were re-run
  only because the ledger row grew three fields.
