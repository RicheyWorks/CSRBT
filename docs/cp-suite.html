The order that keeps plants alive

Not the order the tabs happen to be in. This is the order in which mistakes actually kill plants, commonest first.

Fix the water before anything else

More carnivorous plants die of dissolved solids than of every other cause combined, and it is slow enough that people blame the light. Log your source against the published guidance, and read the tray accumulation arithmetic — ten top-ups at 50 ppm leaves as much dissolved solid in the pot as one watering at 500. That is how growers using genuinely good water still lose plants.

Mix a medium with nothing in it

Nutrient-free is the whole requirement, and it is easier to get wrong than to get right — coir carries salts, most bagged mixes are fertilised, and "peat" at a garden centre often is not. Build by parts, with the nutrient-free flag on.

Before you trust a number

What this suite will and will not tell you

Every figure on both pages falls into one of three buckets, and the pages say which. This is the shortest useful summary of the whole suite.

ships · sourced Water quality guidance The published <160 ppm figure, cited. The stricter ~50 ppm hobby target is labelled a convention, because that is what it is.
ships · measured Trap mechanics, timed Two touches within ~30 s to close a flytrap, five or more to switch on its digestive genes, a bladderwort door open in under 0.5 ms — each with its paper named in the row.
ships · definitional CITES listings Appendix I species named individually, Appendix II genera stated, the #4 annotation explained. These are legal facts, not estimates.
ships · arithmetic Tray accumulation Not a claim about your plants — a multiplication you can check. n top-ups at c ppm is the same dissolved load as one at n×c.
refuses Dormancy temperatures No per-genus number, anywhere on either page. You enter yours and it is recorded with the data.
refuses Dormancy durations and photoperiods Same reason. The fact that a temperate genus needs a dormancy is well established and does ship — the schedule is not.
Why the refusals are the point

The research findingBuilding this suite, the most consequential result was a negative one. There is no citable source for per-genus dormancy temperatures, durations or photoperiods. What exists is a large, confident, mutually inconsistent body of grower convention — regionally assigned, revised whenever somebody's plants die, and repeated between forums until it reads like established fact.

What a lesser tool would doPrint a table anyway. It would look authoritative, it would be wrong in ways nobody could trace, and every record logged against it would inherit the error silently.

What this one doesTakes your value, records where it came from, and tracks against it. You keep the ability to say what your number was and where you got it — which is the only thing that makes two seasons of your own data comparable, and the only honest position when the literature has nothing to offer.

That distinction — between a fact with a source, a convention worth labelling, and a number nobody has measured — is written down as a three-way gate in ADR-031 and applied by every page in the kit.
