Line-point intercept

The quantitative method behind most rangeland monitoring. Drop a pin at fixed intervals along a tape; record what it touches. Cover is then a count, not a guess.

Record a hit

Only the FIRST hit at each point counts as top canopy. Record lower hits separately if your protocol wants foliar layers; record the ground surface (bare soil, litter, rock, moss, biocrust) as the basal hit. Cover for a species = its top-canopy hits ÷ points read.

Method

The scales, the formulas, and where each one stops being trustworthy.

What the entry layer records — and what it does not

The controls are the shared Field Entry Kit, sized for a gloved thumb on a wet tablet. Two of its choices are about the science rather than the ergonomics.

The cover dial is coloured by midpoint, not by position. Cover classes are ordinal, and a row of identical buttons hides that. The colour runs cool to hot with the class midpoint, so Braun-Blanquet 2 and Daubenmire 2 — both 5–25%, both a midpoint of 15% — read as the same colour, and switching scale mid-survey does not silently change what a colour means. The dial is the same instrument the strata and moisture classes use, because those are ordinal too.

Aspect is eight compass points, not a free 0–360 field. A hand compass read while standing on a slope does not give you a degree; recording 227° for "southwest" is a precision that was never measured. If you are reading aspect off a DEM rather than a compass, put the degree in the stand notes.

Blank still means blank. Aspect, slope, moisture, bare ground and litter all start unrecorded and stay out of the export until you touch them — aspect 0° is north, and this sheet will not enter it on your behalf. Tapping a selected dial option again clears it.

Cover scales

Cover classes are ordinal. Averaging class numbers is meaningless; this page converts each class to its midpoint percentage first and works with those, which is standard practice — and still approximate, because a midpoint is not a measurement. Two observers on the same plot commonly differ by a class, so a single-class difference between plots is not a result.

Cover can exceed 100%

Layers overlap, so total cover summed across strata routinely passes 100% in a forest. That is correct, not an error. Within one stratum, a total far above 100% usually means overlapping canopies of individual plants — also legitimate — but a total far above 200% is worth re-checking.

Floristic Quality Assessment

QuantityFormulaWatch out
Mean CΣC / NReported here for natives only, which is the usual convention. Say which convention you used.
FQI (Swink & Wilhelm 1979)mean C × √N Rises with richness, so it is sensitive to plot size and survey effort. Never compare an FQI across different plot sizes.
Adjusted FQI (Miller & Wardrop 2006) 100 × (meanCnat/10) × (√Nnat / √Ntotal) Bounded 0–100 and less effort-sensitive; penalises non-native load explicitly.
Coefficient of conservatismassigned 0–10 by regional panel Not computable from data. It is expert judgement about a species' fidelity to intact habitat, and it is region-specific. Use your state's published list.
FQA is a screening tool, not a verdict. It was built to compare sites within one region using one C-value list, on plots of the same size, surveyed with the same effort. It travels badly across regions and across survey designs. A high FQI does not certify a site and a low one does not condemn it — both are hypotheses about condition that need the rest of your field notes to interpret.

Line-point intercept

QuantityFormulaWatch out
Foliar covertop-canopy hits ÷ points read × 100 Only the first hit at a point is top canopy. Counting every layer as cover double-counts.
Ground coverbasal hits ÷ points read × 100 Bare soil, litter, rock, moss and biocrust are cover types too — record them or your denominator lies.
Precision≈ √(p(1−p)/n) Binomial: the standard error is √(p(1−p)/n). At 100 points a 20% cover estimate gives √(0.20 × 0.80 ÷ 100) = 0.04, so roughly ±4 percentage points — before observer error.

LPI is repeatable in a way ocular cover never is: two observers on the same transect will agree far more closely than two observers eyeballing the same quadrat. It is also slower, and it under-detects rare species — which is why most protocols run LPI for cover and a species inventory for richness.

Wetland indicator status & prevalence index

StatusMeaningWeight
OBLObligate — almost always in wetlands1
FACWFacultative wetland — usually2
FACFacultative — equally likely either way3
FACUFacultative upland — usually not4
UPLObligate upland — almost never5

Prevalence index = Σ(coveri × weighti) ÷ Σcoveri, computed over all strata. An index of 3.0 or below is the threshold the Corps method treats as indicating hydrophytic vegetation.

This is the vegetation indicator only. A wetland determination needs all three: hydrophytic vegetation, hydric soil, and wetland hydrology. A prevalence index below 3.0 satisfies one of the three and settles nothing on its own. Indicator statuses are regional and are revised — take them from the current National Wetland Plant List for your region, which is why this page asks you for them rather than shipping a list that will go stale.

What this page is not

It records one relevé at a time. It does not do classification, ordination, or indicator-species analysis — those need many plots and a statistics package. Export the CSV and take it to R (vegan) or Python when you have a matrix worth ordinating. What this gives you is a clean, method-stated, defensible plot record to feed it.
