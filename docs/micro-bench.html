Method

What a colony count actually counts

CFU/mL = colonies ÷ (volume plated × dilution)

The unit is a colony-forming unit, and the name is the caveat. A CFU is whatever gave rise to one colony. A chain of eight streptococci, a clump of staphylococci, a hypha — each is one CFU. Plate counts therefore underestimate cell number by an amount that depends on how clumpy the organism is, which is why vortexing and the diluent matter and why CFU/mL should never be reported as "cells per mL".

The second and larger caveat: a plate count only sees what grows on that medium, at that temperature, in that atmosphere, in that time. For an environmental sample the culturable fraction is routinely a small percentage of the cells present — the great plate count anomaly, known since the 1930s and confirmed by every sequencing survey since. A plate count of a soil or water sample is a count of the organisms your conditions happened to suit. Say so.

The 30–300 window

Only plates with roughly 30 to 300 colonies are counted. Both ends are real:

  • Below 30 the Poisson error is large. Thirty colonies carries a CV of about 18%; ten carries about 32%. The plate is not wrong, it is imprecise, and averaging several such plates is better than quoting one.
  • Above 300 colonies merge, crowd, and compete for nutrients, so you undercount by a growing and unknowable amount. This is a bias, not noise, and more plates will not fix it.

Plates outside the window are recorded as TFTC (too few to count) and TNTC (too numerous to count) and excluded from the mean. This page shows them, marks them, and leaves them out of the arithmetic rather than silently dropping them — a run where every plate was TNTC is a result about your dilution series, and it should be visible.

Where two adjacent dilutions are both countable, the convention is to use both and average the computed CFU/mL. If they disagree by more than about a factor of two, something is wrong with the series rather than with the organism, and the page says so.

Growth rate from OD

ln(OD) = ln(OD₀) + µt   →   µ = slope  ·  t_d = ln2 / µ

Fitting a straight line to ln(OD) across the exponential phase gives the specific growth rate µ in h⁻¹, and the doubling time follows. Two things to be honest about.

OD is turbidity, not cells. It measures light scattering, and the relationship between scattering and cell number depends on cell size and shape — both of which change with growth phase and medium. An OD-to-CFU conversion is specific to one strain on one instrument in one medium, and it must be calibrated by you before it means anything. This page deliberately offers no such conversion.

OD saturates. Above roughly OD 0.4–0.6 on a typical spectrophotometer, multiple scattering makes the reading fall below linearity, so late points flatten for optical reasons that have nothing to do with the culture. Including them drags µ down. Dilute before reading, or exclude those points and say you did — which is why the phase selection here is manual rather than automatic. Choosing the window is a scientific judgement and the page will not make it for you.

Disc diffusion

Zone diameter is a measurement. Susceptible / intermediate / resistant is an interpretation, and it comes from a breakpoint table that is specific to the organism, the antimicrobial, the disc content, the medium and the method — and that is revised annually by CLSI and EUCAST.

This page ships no breakpoints, and that is deliberate. A hard-coded table would be wrong within a year and dangerously wrong within a few, and software that prints "susceptible" from a stale number is worse than software that prints nothing. Enter the breakpoints from the table you are actually working to, and record which edition it was — the page stores that alongside the result and puts it in the export. With no breakpoints entered you get the measurement and nothing else, which is the honest output.

The method matters as much as the table: Mueller-Hinton at a controlled depth, a 0.5 McFarland inoculum within 15 minutes of preparation, discs applied within 15 minutes of inoculation, 16–18 h at 35 °C in air. Change any of those and the zone changes. A QC strain run alongside is what tells you the method was in control on the day; a result from a run whose QC was out of range is not a result.

Controls

Every count needs an uninoculated control plate from the same batch of medium, incubated alongside. It costs one plate and it is the only thing that distinguishes "my sample contained these organisms" from "my medium did". If the negative control grows, the run is void — not adjusted, void.
