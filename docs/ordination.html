The community matrix

One row per site, one column per species. First column is the site name, first row is the species names. Paste it from a spreadsheet, or stack the matrix rows the Relevé and Stand Sheet already export.

Standardisation

Both of these are on by default in R's vegan::metaMDS, applied silently, and most people who use that function do not know it happened. This page makes them switches and shows you the difference.

Square root pulls in the few very abundant species so the ordination is not just a map of which plot had the most stems. Wisconsin double standardisation divides each species by its own maximum — putting a rare specialist on the same footing as a dominant — then divides each site by its total, so a big plot and a small one are comparable. Neither is neutral. Both change the answer.

Dissimilarity

The ordination

Sites close together held similar communities. That is the only thing the picture says on its own — everything else is on the diagnostics tab.

Every run starts from a different set of random configurations, so the picture may come out rotated or mirrored. That is not instability — NMDS has no preferred orientation. The stress and the start agreement are what tell you whether the solution is stable.

Is the picture worth anything?

A two-dimensional map of a many-dimensional dataset always draws. These are the three things that say whether it drew anything real.

Stress

Kruskal's stress formula 1: √( Σ(d − d̂)² ÷ Σd² ), where d is the distance between two sites in the picture and d̂ is the best monotone fit to their actual dissimilarity. Zero means the picture reproduces the rank order of every pair exactly.

Shepard diagram

Real dissimilarity across, distance in the picture up. The step line is the monotone fit. Points far off it are the pairs the ordination got wrong — a single number cannot tell you which pairs, and this can.

Start agreement

NMDS descends to a local minimum. One low stress from one starting configuration is not evidence that it is the solution. This runs many starts and compares them by Procrustes rotation, which is what vegan does and reports.

Method

What ordination is for

You have twenty plots and thirty species. That is a thirty-dimensional space and nobody can see it. Ordination finds a two-dimensional arrangement of the plots in which the ones with similar communities are close together, so that you can look at the pattern and then ask what environmental variable it lines up with.

It is not a test. It produces no p-value and supports no claim about a difference between groups. The picture is a hypothesis-generating device. Testing whether groups differ in composition is a different tool — PERMANOVA, ANOSIM, or a Mantel test — and this page does not do any of them, because doing one on the same data that suggested the grouping is circular.

NMDS versus PCoA

PCoA (principal coordinates, Gower 1966) is an eigenanalysis: it finds axes that reproduce the actual dissimilarities as faithfully as a straight projection can. Its axes are ordered, each carries a share of the variation, and the answer is unique.

NMDS (Kruskal 1964) throws away the magnitudes and keeps only the rank order of the dissimilarities, then searches for the arrangement that reproduces that order best. It usually fits community data in two dimensions much better than PCoA, because ecological dissimilarity saturates: once two plots share no species, Bray-Curtis is 1 whether they are ten metres or ten kilometres apart, and only the ordering was ever meaningful. The price is that there is no eigenvalue, no percentage of variation, no unique answer, and no interpretation for the axes themselves.

NMDS axes mean nothing. They can be rotated, mirrored or swapped with no change to the fit. Software that labels them NMDS1 and NMDS2 is naming an arbitrary basis, and any sentence of the form “axis 1 represents moisture” is about the arrangement, not the axis. This page draws no axis numbers on an NMDS plot for that reason. What is real is which points sit near which.

Where the stress thresholds come from

The < 0.05 / < 0.10 / < 0.20 bands this page prints are a rule of thumb, not a result. They come from Clarke (1993), Non-parametric multivariate analyses of changes in community structure, Austral Ecology 18:117–143 — and Clarke gave them alongside an explicit warning against applying them mechanically. Stress rises with the number of sites for purely geometric reasons: twenty points are far harder to place than eight, so a stress of 0.15 from forty sites is a better result than 0.15 from ten. The page states the site count next to the band for that reason.

The gate this page applies: the bands ship because they are cited and labelled a convention. What does not ship is any statement that a particular stress makes your conclusion sound. Nothing here knows what you concluded.

Negative eigenvalues

Bray-Curtis is semi-metric: it can violate the triangle inequality, and when it does, PCoA produces negative eigenvalues — axes with imaginary length that cannot be drawn. They are not a bug and they are not rounding. This page counts them and reports how much of the total variation they carry, because a “62% of variation explained” computed over positive eigenvalues only, while a tenth of the variation sits in negative ones, is a number that quietly means something other than what it says.

What this page will not do

It will not run on fewer than four sites, because a two-dimensional ordination of three points is exact by construction and its stress of zero says nothing. It will not fit envelopes or vectors for environmental variables you have not supplied. It will not name your groups.

Nothing here leaves the browser. The matrix, the ordination and every setting stay on your device — no request is made, and there is no server to make one to. The page keeps a copy in this browser's own storage so that closing the tab does not cost you the matrix, and that copy is not a backup: one browser, one device, gone when site data is cleared. The Forget this device’s copy button on the Data tab removes it, and the exports are the durable path.
