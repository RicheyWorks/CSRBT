Decide how many plants you are growing

At least 20 for an inbreeder, 100 for an outbreeder, per variety — and the two halves fail differently. Twenty beans is about catching off-types; a hundred brassicas is about the recessive load heterozygosity has been hiding. The bench carries the mating system for 28 crops so you are not guessing which rule applies.

Work out what your effective population actually is

Nₑ = 4NₘN_f/(Nₘ+N_f). Fifty plants of which five set the pollen is not a population of fifty. The bench prints the inbreeding that costs you per generation and where it lands after ten, and flags when the rarer sex is what caps you.

Isolate — or accept that you did not

The bench cites a distance for the four crops where one is citable and refuses the rest. It does ship the species traps, which are definitional and cost whole crops: every cabbage, kale, broccoli, cauliflower, kohlrabi and Brussels sprout is one species; carrot crosses with wild Queen Anne's lace; beet and chard are the same plant.

Select, and see what it costs

Intensity i from the proportion you keep, and R = i·h²·σ printed as a hypothesis the next generation will test — with h² labelled an assumption rather than a measurement. The harder you select, the fewer parents you keep, and the faster you inbreed. The page says it where the number appears.

Before you trust a number

What this suite will and will not tell you

This is the suite where the refusal is most likely to annoy you, and most likely to be right.

ships · the rule Population minimums 20 inbreeding, 100 outbreeding, per variety — with the mating system and pollination vector carried for 28 crops so the right rule gets applied.
ships · arithmetic Effective population size Nₑ and ΔF are formulas, not estimates. Check them by hand if you like; the bench shows its working.
ships · definitional The species traps That all B. oleracea is one species, that carrot crosses with Queen Anne's lace, that beet and chard are the same plant. Taxonomy, not opinion — and each costs a whole crop.
ships · statistics LSD, CV% and binomial error Standard analyses with their assumptions and failure modes printed beside the number, including where LSD stops being trustworthy.
labelled an assumption Heritability in R = i·h²·σ h² is something you supply or assume, so the response is printed as a hypothesis the next generation will test — never as a forecast.
refuses Isolation distances Except for the four crops with a citable source (NMSU H-262). The rest are regionally assigned and revised, so the bench takes yours and records the provenance.
The tension this suite will not resolve for you

The trade-offSelection intensity and population size pull in opposite directions. Keeping the best 10% of a hundred plants gives you a strong selection differential and ten parents — which is an effective population small enough to accumulate inbreeding at over a percent a generation.

Why no tool can settle itThe right balance depends on how much genetic variation your population has left, how fast you need the trait, whether you can bring in unrelated material later, and what you are willing to lose. Those are your circumstances, not a calculation.

What the bench does insteadShows both numbers on the same screen, at the same time, so the cost is visible while you are choosing rather than three generations later when the vigour has gone. Naming a trade-off honestly is worth more than resolving it dishonestly.

Isolation distances get the same treatment for the same reason, under the three-way gate in ADR-031: a fact with a source ships, a convention ships labelled, and a number nobody has measured is yours to supply.
