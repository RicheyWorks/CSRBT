Pest scouting — walk the W

Walk your field in a W pattern, stop at 10+ points, and count pests on one plant (or leaf) per stop. Tap + at each point. The verdict compares your average to your action threshold — and the spread tells you whether to spot-treat or treat the whole block.

Thresholds vary by crop, pest, and region — use the number from your local extension service or agronomist. The default is only a placeholder.

Pollinator count — ten minutes by the blossoms

Stand by a flowering patch for a set time and tap each visit by type. Diversity — not just the total — is the health signal: a patch worked only by one species is fragile.

Germination test — know your seed lot

Sow a counted sample on damp paper towel, wait the packet's days, count sprouts. The tester gives the lot's rate and how much to over-sow for the stand you want.

Rule of thumb: below ~70%, sow thick or buy fresh seed; below ~50%, the lot is usually not worth the bed space. Old seed of onions, parsnip, and lettuce fades fastest.

Rotation check — don't feed last year's problems

Soil pests and diseases follow plant families, not crop names. Set what each bed grew the last two seasons and what you're planning — the checker flags any family returning within the 3-year rule and suggests what's safe.

The classic guidance is 3–4 years before a family repeats in the same soil — longest for potatoes/tomatoes (Solanaceae) and the cabbage tribe (Brassicaceae). Legumes (Fabaceae) are the classic soil-builders to slot between heavy feeders.
