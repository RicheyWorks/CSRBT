CSRBT · ready to run

Protocol Library

Five complete .eco experiments, ready to copy, edit, and run — each with its hypotheses pre-registered, each graded by the engine. Swap in your own data and predictions.

To run one: copy it into a file (say my-study.eco) and run ./gradlew ecologyExperiment -Pspec=my-study.eco from CSRBT/. The report grades every expect: line ✅/❌/⚠ and exports CSVs plus a printable report.html. Grammar: the .eco Reference. No build? The Interactive Lab reads dropped .eco files into its Workbench.
Protocol 1 · community dynamics

Meadow disturbance

the classic three-act workload: even grazing → a bloom concentrates traffic → seasonal churn

The canonical demo, trimmed to its point: watch diversity collapse when five keys take 90% of the traffic, and prove the two regimes are different communities. One hypothesis is deliberately wrong so you can see what ❌ REFUTED looks like.

meadow-disturbance.eco
name: meadow disturbance study
keys: 100
seed: 42
window: 250

phase: graze   uniform 2000        # even grazing
phase: bloom   hot 2000 5 90        # 5 keys carry 90% of the traffic
phase: seasons churn 1500 55        # turnover: births and deaths

expect: evenness(graze) > 0.9
expect: hill1(bloom) < 20
expect: brayCurtis(graze, bloom) > 0.5
expect: evenness(bloom) > 0.9        # deliberately wrong → REFUTED
expect: evenness(bloom) is uneven
Protocol 2 · field botany

Two-pond comparison

your own species counts, compared site against site — swap in real field data

Pure entered data, no simulation: two plant surveys, narrated by the same instruments and compared pairwise. Replace the counts with your own sites and commit your similarity prediction before you look.

two-ponds.eco
name: two-pond floristic comparison
seed: 7

data: pondA cattail=18 duckweed=44 frogbit=3 azolla=9 rush=6
data: pondB duckweed=51 azolla=2 lily=12 rush=6

note: sampled both ponds after two dry weeks
note(pondA): heavy duckweed mat on the north edge

expect: jaccard(pondA, pondB) <= 0.5     # under half the flora shared
expect: evenness(pondA) is uneven          # duckweed dominates
expect: brayCurtis(pondA, pondB) > 0.4
Protocol 3 · animal behavior

Activity budget, two contexts

an ethogram scored morning vs. afternoon — pairs with the Field Notebook's export

Score the same animal in two contexts (the Field Notebook's tally screen exports these data: lines directly), then test whether the budget really shifted. Bare tokens tally like clipboard marks, so a raw scan record works too.

activity-budget.eco
name: squirrel activity budget, two contexts
seed: 11

data: morning   forage=42 rest=30 groom=12 vigilance=8 locomote=15
data: afternoon forage=20 rest=51 groom=9 vigilance=6 locomote=14
data: scans     forage forage rest forage vigilance rest forage   # raw tallies work too

note: focal animal, 10-minute windows, 30 s scans
note(afternoon): heat of the day — resting up

expect: evenness(morning) is uneven
expect: brayCurtis(morning, afternoon) > 0.3   # the budget shifted
Protocol 4 · genetics

Mendel's bench

peas, flies, chickens — the famous crosses with χ² verdicts, plus Hardy–Weinberg

The complete genetics bench: Mendel's own 1866 counts against 3:1 and 9:3:3:1, incomplete dominance, the two-gene comb cross, a class genotype census against equilibrium, and a bean-lab mark–recapture. Swap in your own F2 counts.

mendels-bench.eco
name: the genetics bench
seed: 1866

cross: Rr x Rr observed 5474 1850      # Mendel's seed shape → χ² vs 3:1
cross: RrYy x RrYy observed 315 108 101 32   # his dihybrid → vs 9:3:3:1
cross: Bb x Bb incomplete              # blue Andalusian: 1:2:1
cross: RrPp x RrPp                     # walnut/rose/pea/single combs
cross: Aa x aa                         # test cross

model: hardyweinberg 298 489 213      # class census: AA Aa aa
model: markrecapture 120 90 30        # the bean lab

note: replace observed counts with your own vial or plot tallies
Protocol 5 · biogeography

The shrinking island

MacArthur–Wilson on the theory bench — area and distance move the equilibrium

Two runs of the island model under different abiotic settings. Run it once as-is, then flip to the small-and-far block and watch the equilibrium richness drop exactly as the crossing rate-curves predict (Lab 6 in the manual walks the theory).

shrinking-island.eco
name: island equilibrium vs area and distance
seed: 1967

# baseline island
factor: area 1.0
factor: distance 1.0
model: island 0.4 0.1 100 0 40       # c, e, mainland pool, S0, steps
model: logistic 0.15 120 5 60

# …then re-run with the island half the size and further out:
#   factor: area 0.5
#   factor: distance 1.5
# prediction: extinction up, immigration down → lower equilibrium richness

note: compare the two runs' island curves — the two-curve logic, on the bench
