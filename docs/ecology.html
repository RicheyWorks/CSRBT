For a practice, not a discipline

Suites

The science kit is organised by method. A suite is organised by what you grow — the same instruments and the same honesty rule, arranged for one bench. ADR-031 sets out why these are separate front doors. Each opens on the order the work actually happens in, and on what that suite refuses to tell you.

suite · carnivorous plants

CP Bench

Water first, because water kills more carnivorous plants than anything else — TDS against the published 160 ppm guidance (California Carnivores), and the tray accumulation arithmetic that explains how growers using good water still lose plants. Nutrient-free media by parts, a per-plant log with provenance, and a dormancy tracker that asks you for the temperature instead of inventing one.

Open the suite → suite · soil & compost

Soil Bench

Compost against 40 CFR 503 and the USDA NOP with the thermophile die-off line drawn, C:N on dry mass rather than buckets, a mix designer, and the USDA texture-by-feel key.

Open the suite → suite · vegetable breeding

Breeding Bench

Minimum population against the mating system — 20 inbreeding, 100 outbreeding — with effective population size and the inbreeding it costs you per generation. Isolation distances cited where a source exists and refused where none does. Selection intensity from the proportion you keep, replicated variety trials with LSD and CV%, and germination tests with the binomial error the sample size actually gives you.

Open the notebook → tablet app · behaviour

Ethogram

Behavioural sampling done to Altmann's rules: declare the sampling and recording rule and the recorder changes to match, states timed and events counted separately, time budgets over observed time, transition matrices, and Cohen's κ for two observers. It refuses to turn one-zero scores into a rate.

Open the ethogram → tablet app · evolution

Selection Log

Selection measured on marked individuals the way a long-term field study measures it: repeated morphometrics with a repeatability that sets the ceiling on everything else, the differential and intensity and gradient, parent–offspring heritability, and R = h²S printed as a hypothesis the next generation will test.

Open the log → tablet app · forest plots

Graduate-level forest plot work in one page: a tap-down tree key for the Pacific Northwest through Tahoe to Utah, tree height from clinometer angles, a stem tally that computes basal area, QMD, SDI and importance values live, photo-anchored notes, and an interaction recorder. Species are a swappable pack — any biome.

The instruments

Standard field methods, oracle-tested

Keys are species, touches are abundance, births and deaths are inserts and removes, and time is counted in operations — so every number reproduces exactly. The same math describes your entered field data.

Run it

From the build, or from the browser

Clone the ecosystem repos as siblings, then from CSRBT/:

./gradlew ecologyFieldDay                 # narrated six-station survey → feeds the lab page
./gradlew ecologyTrace   -Ptrace=your.csv  # replay your own op,key workload
./gradlew ecologyExperiment -Pspec=your.eco # run & grade a pre-registered protocol

Sample inputs to copy from: docs/sample-trace.csv and docs/sample-experiment.eco (a complete worked protocol, with one deliberately-wrong hypothesis so you can see what REFUTED looks like).

No build? You never need Gradle to use the science. The Interactive Lab and the Lab Manual run entirely in a browser, and the lab's transfer box hands you a .eco protocol to keep — see the .eco Reference.

Everything here is deterministic — seeded streams, an operation-indexed clock — and every index is tested against a hand-computed oracle, so you can cite the numbers.
