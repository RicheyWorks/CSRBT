The instruments

Standard field methods, oracle-tested

Keys are species, touches are abundance, births and deaths are inserts and removes, and time is counted in operations — so every number reproduces exactly. The same math describes your entered field data.

StationMeasuresMethod
The meadowDiversity & evennessShannon, Simpson, Hill, Pielou (1948–73)
The censusSurvivorship & growthDeevey life tables, logistic growth (1838, 1947)
The archipelagoMetapopulationLevins patch dynamics (1969)
The survey gridSpatial patternQuadrat dispersion, Morisita (1959)
The islandTurnover at capacityMacArthur–Wilson biogeography (1967)
Genetics benchHeredityPunnett squares, χ², Hardy–Weinberg, mark–recapture

The full catalogue, station by station, is in the Field Guide.

Run it

From the build, or from the browser

Clone the ecosystem repos as siblings, then from CSRBT/:

./gradlew ecologyFieldDay                 # narrated six-station survey → feeds the lab page
./gradlew ecologyTrace   -Ptrace=your.csv  # replay your own op,key workload
./gradlew ecologyExperiment -Pspec=your.eco # run & grade a pre-registered protocol

Sample inputs to copy from: docs/sample-trace.csv and docs/sample-experiment.eco (a complete worked protocol, with one deliberately-wrong hypothesis so you can see what REFUTED looks like).

No build? You never need Gradle to use the science. The Interactive Lab and the Lab Manual run entirely in a browser, and the lab's transfer box hands you a .eco protocol to keep — see the .eco Reference.

Everything here is deterministic — seeded streams, an operation-indexed clock — and every index is tested against a hand-computed oracle, so you can cite the numbers.
